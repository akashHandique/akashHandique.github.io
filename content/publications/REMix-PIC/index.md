abstract: "Pacific Island Countries are among the world's most dependent on imported fossil fuels for energy supply, which account for a considerable share of their gross domestic product. Existing island energy research concentrates on the Atlantic, Mediterranean, Indian Ocean, and East Asian regions, presenting a gap for islands in the Pacific. Consequently, quantitative energy transition pathways for these islands remain absent from the literature. This study introduces REMix-PIC, an open-source linear optimization model that integrates the electricity, heat, and transport sectors for 14 Pacific Islands at hourly resolution to 2050 across four scenarios spanning national self-sufficiency, inter-island sustainable fuel trade, and imports from New Zealand. Results highlight that electrification of the energy system requires power generation capacity to expand from 1.4 to 62 GW and electricity supply from 5 to 87 TWh, corresponding to 13.5% annual growth. Although annual system costs rise by 24%, the levelized cost of final energy falls by 25% to $60/MWh, driven by 60-80% reductions in electricity and heat supply costs. The transition requires 8.9% of gross domestic product annually, below the 12% projected under continued fossil fuel dependence. All countries except Nauru can produce sustainable maritime and aviation fuels domestically; however, inter-island trade and 12 TWh of ammonia imported from New Zealand reduce transition costs by up to 3%, with Vanuatu, Kiribati, and Tonga emerging as fuel exporting hubs. Batteries provide daily balancing while hydrogen storage enables seasonal balancing. These insights provide a quantitative basis for national energy strategies, investment planning, and regional cooperation across the Pacific."
